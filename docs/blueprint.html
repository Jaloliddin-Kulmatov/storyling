Product & architecture blueprint · Draft 1

Storyling: learn languages through stories you can almost read

What existing story apps get wrong, the features that fix it, and how to build Storyling as a website and an iOS/Android app from one codebase.

Date 6 Oct 2026Languages 10 at launchPlatforms Web, iOS, Android, browser extensionDesign Storyling Design canvas

01Summary

Story apps for language learners fall into three groups. Curated graded readers (Readle, TTMIK Stories, Todaii, Du Chinese, Satori Reader) have good content but cover one language each, grade by a level label instead of what you know, and can't read your own texts. Import readers (LingQ, Readlang) read anything and track your words, but reviewers call them cluttered and buggy. AI story apps (StoryLearning app, Langua, ReadYours) generate endless personal stories, but even their makers say the stories are simple, and quality checking is unclear.

Storyling combines the strengths of all three and adds what none of them has: every text is fitted to the words you actually know, stories bring your new words back on a schedule, and reading flows into listening, speaking and retelling. It ships on web, iOS and Android with one subscription for all 10 languages.

Pillar 1

Just-right reading

Each text shows how many of its words you know. Any story or import can be rewritten to your level.

Pillar 2

Stories that remember you

Series and personal stories reuse your learning words at growing intervals, so reading becomes review.

Pillar 3

Read → listen → speak

Synced audio, shadowing with pronunciation feedback, and a short retell after every story.

Pillar 4

Any text, anywhere

Web pages, EPUB, subtitles and photos of real pages become readable lessons, online or offline.

Pillar 5

Trust by default

Quality badges on content, labeled audio, honest feedback, clear trials, one plan for every language.

04Product pillars and features

Release tags: MVP first public beta · v1 public launch · v2 within a year · later.

1

Just-right reading

Fixes: levels that are only labels, advanced learners running out of content.
  • MVPPersonal word model. Every word has a chance-you-know-it score that updates from what you read, tap, review and say. No manual "mark as known" chores.
  • MVP3-minute placement. A yes/no vocabulary check across frequency bands, with made-up words mixed in to catch guessing. Seeds the word model on day one.
  • MVPFit badge on every text. "You know 96% · 7 new words". The library is sorted so the best fits come first.
  • v1Level slider. Easier / Original / Harder for any story or import. The rewrite keeps names and plot, and shows which words changed.
  • v1Pre-reading warm-up. The 3–5 most important new words, with audio, before you start.
2

Stories that remember you

Fixes: flashcard fatigue, one-off stories with no continuity.
  • MVPSeries with recurring characters. Episodes of 2–5 minutes, 5–20 episodes per series, built so vocabulary repeats across episodes.
  • v1Story for me. Pick a genre and a setting; the story uses your due words and your interests. Labeled "AI-written · auto-checked".
  • v1Reading counts as review. Meeting a due word in a story and understanding it (no tap) counts as a successful review, so you do fewer flashcards.
  • v1Encounter meter. Each word shows how often you've met it in context (for example 4 of ~10), because learning from reading needs many meetings.
  • v2Daily episode. One new episode generated overnight for each subscriber, using that day's due words.
3

Read → listen → speak → retell

Fixes: passive reading with no output, unreliable speech feedback.
  • MVPSynced audio. Sentence highlighting, 0.75× without pitch drop, repeat a sentence, tap a word to hear it.
  • v1Listen mode. Background audio with lock-screen controls and a pause after each sentence for shadowing. Works offline.
  • v1Shadowing with feedback. Record a sentence and see which sounds were off. A score is shown only when recognition is confident.
  • v2Retell. Say or type 2–4 sentences about the story. Feedback praises what you got right, corrects up to 3 things, and links each to a word or pattern from the story.
  • v2Talk to a character. A short conversation with a character from the story, limited to the story's vocabulary.
4

Any text, anywhere

Fixes: closed catalogs, no desktop or no offline.
  • MVPPaste text or a link. Tokenized, glossed and given a fit badge in seconds. Private to you.
  • v1Browser extension. Storyling word colors on any web page; one click saves it to your library.
  • v1EPUB and subtitles. Read a whole book chapter by chapter, or a show's subtitles before watching.
  • v2Photo import. Take a photo of a menu, sign or book page. On-device text recognition turns it into a lesson.
  • MVPOffline first. Downloaded stories, audio and reviews work on a plane, free tier included; progress syncs later.
5

Help exactly where you're stuck

Fixes: weak grammar explanations, removed discussions, scripts that block reading.
  • MVPScript controls per language. Furigana only on kanji you don't know, pinyin and tones, Korean romanization off by default, Russian stress marks, German noun gender colors, simplified or traditional Chinese.
  • v1Ask the text. "Why is it 에게 here?" Answers come from an AI tutor grounded in the sentence, with the pattern linked to other story examples.
  • v1Pattern cards. Each grammar pattern collects every sentence you've read that uses it.
  • v1Word notes. Add your own nuance notes and mnemonics to any word; they show on review cards.
  • v2Sentence questions. Ask and answer questions on a sentence with other learners, moderated, with editor answers pinned.
6

Trust and together

Fixes: surprise charges, hidden AI content, learning alone.
  • MVPQuality badges. "Editor-reviewed" or "AI-written · auto-checked" on every story; an audio label on every player.
  • MVPReport a problem on any word, sentence or translation. Fixes ship to everyone.
  • MVPYour data is yours. Export words to CSV or Anki; delete your account in settings.
  • v2Classes. Teachers assign stories, set a coverage target, and see who finished and who struggled with which words.
  • v2Reading clubs. Small groups read the same series weekly, with shared highlights.
  • laterExplanations in more languages than English, a kids mode, and more learning languages (Arabic, Hindi, Turkish…).

Accessibility built in

  • Word states differ in shape as well as color (amber underline for new, blue dotted box for learning), so they work for colorblind readers.
  • Adjustable text size, line spacing and a dyslexia-friendly font option; full screen-reader labels; 44 px touch targets.
  • Every audio track has its text; every image has alt text; motion respects reduced-motion settings.

05Core user journeys

First 5 minutes
  1. Pick a language (10 cards).
  2. 3-minute placement check.
  3. Pick 3 interests.
  4. First story opens at about 95% fit, audio ready.
  5. Finish → "You met 6 new words" → save 3.
Daily habit (10–15 min)
  1. Home shows today's plan: 1 episode, due reviews, 1 speaking task.
  2. Read the next episode; due words appear in it.
  3. Quick retell or shadow 3 sentences.
  4. Remaining flashcards (fewer, because reading counted).
Real-world text
  1. Share a web article to Storyling, or take a photo of a page.
  2. See its fit: 81%, too hard.
  3. Slide to Easier: 95%.
  4. Read the easier version, then the original.
Commute
  1. Downloaded series plays in Listen mode, phone locked.
  2. Pause after each sentence to shadow.
  3. "Didn't catch it" taps mark sentences to read later.
Teacher
  1. Create a class, share a code.
  2. Assign "Episode 3" with a 95% target.
  3. Dashboard: completion, quiz results, hardest words.

06The learning engine

Four parts: a word model, a coverage calculator, a scheduler, and a recommender. They live in a shared TypeScript package, so web, app and server compute the same numbers, even offline.

Word model

Each (learner, lemma) pair stores a probability that the learner knows the word. It starts from the placement test and the word's frequency rank, then updates from events.

prior(word) = sigmoid( a · (V_est − rank(word)) / V_est ) // V_est = estimated vocabulary size from placement update = logit(p) += w_event // per event, then clamp w_event : read past without tapping +0.35 · tapped −0.6 · review Good +0.8 / Again −1.0 used correctly in retell +1.0 · shadowed clearly +0.2 known : p ≥ 0.90 learning : saved by the learner, or 0.35 ≤ p < 0.90 after a tap

The weights are starting guesses. Fit them on real event logs once there are a few thousand learners (logistic regression on "tapped next time or not").

Placement (3 minutes)

  • 40 items: real words sampled from frequency bands (1k, 2k, 3k, 5k, 8k), plus 10 made-up words that look real.
  • The learner answers "I know it" or "Not sure". Saying yes to made-up words lowers the estimate (the standard yes/no-test correction).
  • Output: estimated vocabulary size and a CEFR/HSK/JLPT/TOPIK band, shown honestly as an estimate.

Coverage and fit

coverage(text, user) = Σ p_known(lemma_i) / N // over word tokens; names and numbers excluded fit: ≥ 98% Easy · 95–98% Just right · 90–95% Stretch · < 90% Too hard → offer "Make it easier"

The 95–98% band comes from reading research: Hu and Nation (2000) found 98% known words to be the comfortable level for unassisted reading of fiction. Storyling allows a little below that because tap-to-translate and audio help.

Scheduler

  • FSRS (open-source spaced-repetition algorithm, ts-fsrs) for flashcards.
  • Narrative review: when a due word appears in a story and the learner reads past it without tapping, that counts as a "Good" review. Tapping it counts as "Again".
  • Daily plan balances new reading, due reviews and one speaking task, sized to the learner's goal (5 / 10 / 20 min).

Recommender

score = 1.0 · fit_closeness(coverage, 0.965) + 0.6 · series_continuity + 0.4 · interest_match + 0.3 · due_words_in_text + 0.2 · freshness − 0.5 · recently_abandoned

A simple weighted score is easy to explain and debug. Swap in a learned ranker later, once there's data.

Automatic checks (every AI text)

  • Level fit: coverage against the target band's word list, new-word budget (2–5%), sentence length limits.
  • Grammar and naturalness: a second AI pass reviews the text as a native editor would and lists problems; any "high" problem fails the text.
  • Meaning: back-translation compared with the brief or the original (for rewrites), so plot and names are kept.
  • Safety: age-appropriate content filter; no real private people.
  • Tokenization sanity: every token maps to a lemma with a gloss; unknown tokens send the text to review.

Dictionary data

Seed lemmas and glosses from open dictionaries (Wiktionary, JMdict, CC-CEDICT, the National Institute of Korean Language's learner dictionary), then fill gaps with AI glosses that editors check. Most are CC BY-SA licensed, which requires attribution and share-alike for the dictionary data. Get a licensing review before launch.

08AI usage

All AI features call Claude through the official Anthropic SDK from the server, never from the client. Default model: claude-opus-5-5, with effort tuned per feature.

FeatureWhen it runsSettingsCost controls
Catalog episode draftsOffline, in batchesEffort high; structured output (title, sentences, target words used)Message Batches API (50% cheaper); style guide cached as a prompt prefix
Glosses and sentence translationsAt publish or importEffort low; structured output keyed by token indexDone once per text and cached for all learners
Level rewriteOn demandEffort medium; allowed-word list in the prompt; up to 3 attempts against the checkerResult cached per (text, band)
Story for me / daily episodeOn demand, or overnightEffort medium; learner's due words + interestsOvernight episodes via Batch API; daily quota by plan
Ask the textLive chatEffort low; streaming; story text as a cached prefixPrompt caching; answers to common questions reused
Retell feedbackAfter a storyEffort medium; structured output (praise, up to 3 corrections, linked words)Short inputs; one call per retell
Content checkerEvery AI textEffort medium; returns a list of problems with severityRuns only on new text
  • Refusals: enable the API's server-side fallback option and always check the stop reason before using output.
  • Grounding: the tutor only receives the current story and the learner's level. Learner-imported text is passed as clearly marked data, never as instructions.
  • Evals: each AI feature has a fixed test set graded by native reviewers (level fit, correctness, naturalness, story quality). No prompt or model change ships without beating the current version.

Rough cost of a 250-word personal story with glosses: a few cents of AI usage at Claude Opus 5.5 prices ($4 / $20 per million input / output tokens), about half that through the Batch API. Measure real token use in the beta before setting plan limits.

How a reading session flows

sequenceDiagram
  participant U as Learner
  participant C as App (local SQLite)
  participant S as Sync
  participant A as API
  U->>C: Open episode
  C->>C: Coverage from local word model
  U->>C: Tap word / read past words
  C->>C: Append events, update p_known
  U->>C: Finish story
  C->>C: Narrative reviews, FSRS updates
  C-->>S: Upload events when online
  S-->>A: Server recomputes, resolves conflicts
  A-->>C: New daily plan + next episode

Sync rule: clients never overwrite each other's word scores. They upload append-only events, and the server replays them in timestamp order to compute the official state. This makes phone + laptop use safe.

Security and privacy

  • Row-level security: a learner can read only their own words, imports, recordings and notes.
  • Voice recordings are deleted after scoring unless the learner keeps them.
  • GDPR: data export (JSON, CSV, Anki), account deletion within 30 days, EU data region option.
  • Kids mode (later) needs parental consent flows (COPPA, GDPR-K) and no community features.
  • Learner text sent to AI is marked as data, never instructions, to block prompt injection from imported pages.

11Web and app specifics

Web
  • Every free episode has a public, server-rendered page ("Easy Korean story: At the convenience store") for search traffic, with a "Read with your level" button.
  • The reader uses native HTML <ruby> for furigana and pinyin.
  • Keyboard shortcuts: ← → sentences, Space play, 1/2/3 word state.
  • Installable PWA; offline library through SQLite WASM.
  • Wide screens: text column + word panel + series rail.
iOS and Android
  • React Native has no <ruby>, so the reader lays out tokens as wrapping views with readings above. Profile it early on long CJK texts.
  • Background audio with lock-screen controls (react-native-track-player).
  • Downloads: stories + audio + glosses packed per series.
  • Share sheet: "Read in Storyling" from any app; camera import.
  • Widgets: today's plan and due count; gentle reminders the learner sets.
Shared
  • packages/core: word model, coverage, FSRS and recommender, with identical results on every platform.
  • packages/reader: selection, audio sync and script controls as a state machine; each platform only draws it.
  • packages/ui-tokens: one source of colors, type and spacing for Tailwind and the native theme.

  • Calm motivation. Streaks are optional and forgiving (freeze days); no leaderboards by default.

18Testing and quality

  • Unit tests for packages/core: word model updates, coverage, FSRS scheduling, recommender ordering.
  • Golden tests per language: 500 sentences with expected tokens, lemmas and readings; run in CI on every NLP change.
  • AI evals: fixed prompts per feature, graded by native reviewers plus an AI grader checked against them.
  • End-to-end tests: Playwright on web; Maestro on iOS and Android (onboarding, read, review, offline, purchase).
  • Release gates: crash-free ≥ 99.5%, no failing golden tests, AI eval score not below the current version.
