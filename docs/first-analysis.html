2. What each one gets right

Readle

Daily habit, low friction

  • Stories are short enough to finish in 3–5 minutes, so it fits a commute.
  • Fresh content every day gives people a reason to open the app.
  • Real-world topics make the learner feel they are reading "actual German".
  • The free tier rotates stories, which works as a trial without a paywall wall.
  • Anki export keeps power users happy.
TTMIK Stories

Curriculum depth and audio quality

  • Levels follow a known curriculum, so learners trust the grading.
  • Human voice actors are a selling point they put in the store listing.
  • Sentence-level translation helps beginners who are stuck on grammar, not words.
  • Writing practice turns reading into output.
  • TTMIK's YouTube, podcast and textbook audience feeds the app.

Shared pattern. Both apps use the same core loop: pick a story at your level → read with tap-to-translate and audio → quiz → save words → review with SRS. This loop is the baseline. A new product needs it too, and has to win on something else.

3. Gaps you can win on

Readle weaknesses
  • Reviewers complain about AI voices replacing human narration.
  • B2 and C1 content is added slowly, so advanced learners outgrow it.
  • Quiz bugs and slow fixes give some users the feeling the app is abandoned.
  • News-only tone. There is little fiction, serialized story or dialogue.
TTMIK Stories weaknesses
  • One language, so a learner of Korean and Japanese needs two apps.
  • $16.99/month is expensive compared with most language apps.
  • Limited offline use, according to review roundups.
  • Content is closed. You can't read your own texts with its tools.

Things neither app does

  1. Known-word tracking. Neither app tells you "you know 96% of the words in this story". LingQ does this, but its interface is cluttered. A clean version is an opening.
  2. Bring your own text. Paste an article, a K-drama script or an e-book chapter and get the same tap-to-translate, audio and vocab tools.
  3. Explanations in the learner's own language. Both explain things in English. Learners whose first language is Uzbek, Russian, Turkish, Arabic or another underserved language get little support. This is a real niche, especially for Korean, where Central Asia has a large learner base.
  4. Story series and characters. Serialized stories with recurring characters bring people back better than standalone articles.
  5. Social reading. Book clubs, shared highlights, or a teacher assigning a story to a class.

4. Product concept

Positioning: one reading app for several languages, which always picks stories you can understand. It tracks every word you know, recommends stories at 95–98% known-word coverage, and explains things in your own language.

The 95–98% target is the core idea. Research on extensive reading (Paul Nation and others) found that learners read comfortably and pick up new words from context when about 98% of the running words are already known. Graded levels like "A2" or "Level 4" only approximate this. Tracking each learner's own vocabulary measures it directly.

5. Content strategy

Content is the most expensive part and the hardest to copy, so plan it first. TTMIK has staff writers and voice actors. A small team has to use a pipeline instead.

  1. Choose 2 launch languages. Korean plus one more you can quality-check (Japanese, Spanish or English). Korean alone competes directly with TTMIK. Korean with Uzbek/Russian explanations has almost no competition.
  2. Build level word lists from frequency data and exam lists: CEFR (Profile / Kelly lists), TOPIK vocabulary, HSK 3.0, JLPT. A story's level is the share of its words that fall inside each list.
  3. Draft with an LLM under strict constraints (allowed vocabulary, grammar points, length), then have a native speaker edit every story. Never publish unedited AI text. Readle's reviews show users notice and dislike quality drops.
  4. Audio: use high-quality neural TTS for the MVP and record human audio for the most popular stories later. Say clearly in the app which is which.
  5. Public-domain texts (folk tales, classic short stories) work well at upper levels with modern glossing.

Core data model

TableKey fields
languagescode, name, script, tokenizer
lemmaslanguage, dictionary form, part of speech, frequency rank, level tags (A1, TOPIK 1…)
storieslanguage, title, level, series, body, audio URL, status (draft / reviewed / live)
story_tokensstory, position, surface form, lemma, sentence index, audio start/end
glosseslemma, explanation language, meaning, example
user_wordsuser, lemma, state (new / learning / known / ignored), FSRS fields, source sentence
reading_sessionsuser, story, started, finished, words read, quiz score

A story's coverage for a user is the share of its story_tokens whose lemma is "known" in user_words. Calculate it in one SQL query and cache it per user per story.

7. Business model

  • Free: 3 stories a week, full reader, up to 100 saved words. Enough to form a habit.
  • Premium: about $7.99/month or $49.99/year. That is under both competitors ($9.99 and $16.99 a month).
  • Regional pricing: much lower prices in Central Asia, South and Southeast Asia, and Latin America, where many Korean learners live.
  • Later: school and teacher plans, and a revenue share with content creators.

How people will find it: SEO pages for each free story (people search for "easy Korean story for beginners"), short reading videos on TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts, and Telegram channels for learners in Uzbek and Russian.

8. Roadmap

Weeks 1–3Validate
Interview 15–20 learners. Publish 20 free stories as plain web pages with a simple tap-to-translate script, and share them in learner communities. Measure how many finish a story and come back.
Weeks 4–10MVP
Accounts, reader with word states, audio highlighting, FSRS review, and 150 stories in 2 languages. Private beta with 100 users.
Weeks 11–16Launch
Paywall, SEO story pages, coverage-based recommendations, PWA install. Public launch on Product Hunt and Reddit (r/Korean, r/LearnJapanese, r/languagelearning).
Months 5–9Grow
Text and URL import, serialized stories, offline mode, native mobile apps, a third language, and human audio for top stories.

9. Metrics and risks

Track these
  • Story completion rate (target above 60%)
  • Day-7 and day-30 retention
  • Words moved to "known" per week
  • Free-to-paid conversion (3–5% is typical for consumer subscriptions)
  • Reviews done per day versus stories read (a balance check)
Risks
  • Content quality. AI drafts that sound unnatural. Fix: a native editor on every story.
  • Bad tokenization in Korean and Japanese breaks word tracking. Test the analyzer on your own stories early.
  • Copyright. Don't copy competitors' stories or news articles. User imports stay private to that user.
  • Scope. Too many languages too soon. Make two languages excellent first.
